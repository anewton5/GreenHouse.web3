1. Strategic Direction: The "Intermittent Liquidity" Model
In 2026, the European Commission is actively consulting on Intermittent Multilateral Trading Platforms. This is the "sweet spot" for Series A/B companies.  

The Scope: Instead of a 24/7 exchange (which small companies hate due to volatility), GreenHouse should facilitate "Liquidity Windows" (e.g., quarterly 1-week windows).

Demand: Founders want to provide liquidity to early employees and investors without the compliance burden of a full public listing.

The Edge: Use smart contracts to automate Right of First Refusal (ROFR) and "Drag-along/Tag-along" rights, which are currently manual legal nightmares in cross-border deals.

2. Solving the "Cross-Border" Legal Friction
The primary constraint in Europe isn't just the tech; it’s the Notary and Registry bottleneck.

Direction: Don't just tokenise the equity directly (which often requires a notary in jurisdictions like Germany). Use a Luxembourg or Irish SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle) structure.

The Model: GreenHouse issues "Participation Notes" or "Depository Receipts" on-chain that represent a claim on the underlying shares held by the SPV.

Value Add: This allows a French Family Office to invest in a German startup using a familiar, cross-border compliant instrument without needing to fly to a Berlin notary.

3. Capability Expansion: The "Compliance-as-a-Service" Stack
Since you are targeting HNWIs and Family Offices, your platform's "site capability" should focus on Wealth Aggregation and Reporting.

The Problem: Family offices hate having 15 different PDF statements for 15 different private placements.

The Solution: Provide an on-chain reporting dashboard that pulls real-time valuation data (if available) and tax-ready reporting for different EU jurisdictions.

Demand: In 2026, the Financial Data Access (FiDA) regulation makes data sharing easier. GreenHouse should act as a "Consolidator" for private market holdings.  

4. Roadmap: From "Private Placement" to "DLT Pilot Regime"
Phase 1 (Current): Operate under the "Professional Investor" exemption (Prospectus Regulation). No heavy license is needed, but you are limited to "Verified Accredited" users.

Phase 2 (Medium Term): Apply for the DLT Pilot Regime authorization. This allows you to combine the roles of a CSD (Central Securities Depository) and a Trading Venue.

The Advantage: This removes the need for an external bank to "settle" the cash side. You can settle the trade and the asset simultaneously (Atomic Settlement), reducing "settlement risk" to zero.  

5. Competitive Positioning: The "Quality Gate"
Unlike retail platforms like Republic or Crowdcube, GreenHouse's value is in curation.

Direction: Implement a "Lead Investor" model where a reputable VC or Family Office "anchors" the deal on-chain.

Potential: In 2026, AI-driven due diligence tools are standard. GreenHouse could offer an "Automated Data Room" feature that uses AI to flag discrepancies in a startup's cap table or legal docs before the placement goes live.
